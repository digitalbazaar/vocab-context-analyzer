Golden-Set Labeling

What you're doing here

You are creating the expert standard that an LLM design-scoring layer will be measured against. Each case is a JSON-LD vocabulary and its @context. Automated rules have already caught the objective defects (missing definitions, unmapped terms, broken hierarchies — shown in the amber box, do not re-report those). Your job is the layer the rules cannot reach: naming quality, definition quality, modeling choices, and conceptual gaps.

The screen

  • Left sidebar — the cases, grouped by cohort. The dot shows your verdict; #n shows your rank. Case ids are deliberately anonymized (case-07, …) so nothing about a case's origin can influence you — judge only the content.
  • Middle — the case itself: the raw JSON-LD documents (@context first, then the vocabulary), with a toggle to a flattened term summary.
  • Right — your labels for the selected case, with any rule-caught findings shown above them (only when there are some).

overall verdict — good / bad / borderline

Your holistic judgment of the design, counting everything — the rule-caught defects in the amber box are part of the picture and can make a case bad on their own. (The "don't re-report" rule below applies only to issue rows, never to your verdict.) borderline is a real answer for genuinely debatable cases — it is not a way to avoid deciding. A borderline verdict needs at least one issue (below) explaining the tension. Click a selected verdict again to unset it.

design rank

Orders the cases within a cohort from best design (1) to worst. Use the rank button on a cohort header in the sidebar and drag the list into order. Only within-cohort order matters — it feeds a rank-correlation metric.

subjective issues

Term-level problems, one row each: pick the term, a category, and write a note. The four categories:

  • naming — clarity/consistency of a term's name. "verb-phrase predicate, inconsistent with noun siblings"
  • definition — a definition that exists but is poor (circular, restates the label). Absent definitions are the rules' job, not yours.
  • modeling — wrong granularity or structure. "a document concern modeled as a Person attribute"
  • coverage — a concept the vocabulary obviously needs but gives no way to express.

Two non-negotiables

  • No deferrals. "Needs expert input" is a failed label — you are the expert input. Genuine uncertainty = borderline + an issue explaining why.
  • Every note must be actionable. "Bad name" is not enough; "rename to a noun to match its siblings" is.

Saving and handing off

Everything autosaves into your checkout at test/fixtures/golden/labels.json (watch the top-right indicator). Quit and relaunch anytime — your work resumes. When you're done: commit that file on your branch, push, and notify the integrating engineer — they import your labels into the golden set with one command.

Drag to order — 1 = best design. Applying assigns dense ranks to every case in the cohort.
